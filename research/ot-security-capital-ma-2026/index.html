2026 OT Security Capital & M&A Analysis

An analysis of funding, mergers, acquisitions and partnerships among OT security vendors from January through November 2026.

Port 502 Research tracked funding rounds, mergers and acquisitions, and partnerships involving OT security vendors announced between January and November 2026, using company announcements and regulatory filings. Data as of November 27, 2026.

01

Market overview

Demand for OT security continues to grow as industrial networks converge with IT systems and threats to critical infrastructure intensify. MarketsandMarkets projects the market will expand from $27.4 billion in 2026 to $58.9 billion by 2031.

Operational exposure remains high. In a 2026 SANS survey, 34% of industrial organizations reported an OT incident, detection slowed for the first time, and only 15% could sustain manual operations for more than two days. These results indicate that resilience, and not only detection, remains a gap for many operators.

Capital activity in 2026 did not translate into a wave of new entrants. Investment was concentrated in acquisitions of established vendors, while venture funding remained modest and limited to a small number of companies.

This report examines capital raised, mergers and acquisitions, and partnerships announced between January and November 2026.

02

Key findings

  • 01Acquisition activity far exceeded venture investment. Four transactions valued at $1 billion or more closed or were announced, led by ServiceNow’s approximately $7.75 billion cash acquisition of Armis and Accenture’s Dragos transaction at a combined value of roughly $4.175 billion.
  • 02Venture capital was concentrated in two companies. Claroty’s $150 million Series F at a $3 billion valuation and Kai’s $125 million launch accounted for most of the capital raised by OT-focused companies. Most other rounds were below $30 million.
  • 03The largest acquirers came from outside OT security. Buyers included an industrial conglomerate, an enterprise software platform, a global consultancy and a quantum computing company, which suggests OT security is increasingly valued as a component of broader platforms.
  • 04Few large independents remain. Claroty is the last large independent platform, and OPSWAT is the clearest IPO candidate among the remaining independent vendors.

03

Venture and private capital

Venture funding in 2026 was concentrated in a small number of companies. Most disclosed rounds were below $30 million, and a single company accounted for the majority of capital raised by established vendors.

Claroty raised a $150 million Series F at a $3 billion valuation on January 22, 2026, led by Golub Growth. It was the largest round of the year among established OT vendors, although roughly a third of the proceeds went to employee share sales rather than to the company.

The second-largest round went to a new entrant with experienced founders. Kai launched with $125 million led by Evolution Equity Partners to build an agentic platform for IoT and OT environments. Its co-founders include a Claroty co-founder, which indicates investors favored teams with a prior track record in the category.

Smaller rounds were concentrated in AI and software integrity. Frenos and Indurex raised capital for AI-driven OT analysis, Finite State for firmware and supply chain security, and Copia for OT code management.

Private equity also entered the mid-market. Banneker Partners took a majority stake in Industrial Defender, the company’s first institutional capital.

04

Mergers and acquisitions

Consolidation defined the OT security market in 2026, with most established vendors acquired by companies outside the category. Four transactions valued at $1 billion or more closed or were announced, and the acquirers included an industrial conglomerate, an enterprise software platform, a global consultancy and a quantum computing company.

Mitsubishi Electric completed its acquisition of Nozomi Networks in January in a deal valued at about $1 billion. In April, ServiceNow closed its acquisition of Armis for approximately $7.75 billion, financed through a combination of cash on hand and debt.

Accenture completed the largest OT-specific transaction. On June 18, it announced a majority stake in Dragos at a $3.25 billion valuation alongside the full acquisition of runZero and NetRise, for a combined enterprise value of roughly $4.175 billion. Dragos also expanded on its own by acquiring Phosphorus in June. Phosphorus had raised approximately $65 million in funding.

IonQ completed the year’s final major transaction on November 4, acquiring Elisity in an all-stock deal. IonQ issued $1.15 billion of common stock at closing, with up to $150 million in additional consideration tied to revenue milestones through January 2028.

Smaller transactions involved private equity and specialist buyers. Banneker Partners acquired a majority stake in Industrial Defender, and Landis+Gyr agreed to sell Rhebo, its OT security division, to Everfield Germany, a European buy-and-hold software investor.

Remaining independent vendors

Claroty is the largest OT security vendor that remains independent, with its most recent round valuing the company at $3 billion.

TXOne Networks, OPSWAT, Waterfall Security, Cyolo, Dispel and Xage Security also remain independent. OPSWAT appears to be the most likely IPO candidate, having publicly discussed its Tampa expansion and IPO preparations in June.

Remote access and data diode vendors account for most of the remaining independents. These are the least consolidated segments of the market, which suggests they are likely areas for future acquisition activity.

05

Partnerships and integrations

Vendors that remained independent were active in partnerships throughout 2026. Most agreements fell into two categories: distribution relationships with automation and industrial companies, and technical integrations with the newly consolidated platforms.

Distribution agreements extended vendor reach through established industrial channels. OPSWAT and Emerson signed a global strategic reseller agreement in April, and ABB and Cydome partnered to integrate voyage optimization with maritime cybersecurity to strengthen risk management for shipping companies.

Remote access vendors were the most active integrators. Dispel announced three integrations during the year, expanding its work with Nozomi Networks and TXOne Networks at S4x26 in February and adding an integration with Armis, now part of ServiceNow, in August.

Several vendors also partnered with peers. Industrial Defender and Fortress Information Security developed an AI-driven integration intended to help clients identify and remediate threats faster, and Claroty partnered with Frenos on AI-driven OT security validation.

06

Conclusion

The 2026 data points to a market moving away from independent specialists and toward OT security delivered as part of larger platforms, consulting practices and industrial equipment portfolios. For asset owners, this shift is likely to change how OT security is purchased. Capabilities once evaluated as standalone products will increasingly arrive bundled with IT service management, consulting engagements or automation hardware. That may simplify procurement, but it can also reduce vendor neutrality and buyer leverage.

For acquired vendors, the next test is integration. Their new owners paid for installed bases, asset data and domain expertise, and the return on these deals will depend on whether that expertise and product focus are retained inside organizations whose core business lies elsewhere. Customers should watch roadmap commitments, support models and pricing as integration progresses.

For the remaining independents, the room for differentiated positioning is narrowing. Remote access, data diodes and recovery remain fragmented, and the partnership activity seen in 2026 suggests these vendors are positioning themselves as interoperable components within consolidated ecosystems. The same positioning may make them attractive targets as larger platforms look to fill capability gaps.

Several developments will indicate whether this trajectory continues into 2027. These include whether Claroty pursues a public listing or a sale, whether OPSWAT proceeds with an IPO, and whether additional platform or networking vendors follow ServiceNow and Accenture into the category. The concentration of new venture capital in AI-driven analysis also suggests that the next generation of OT security companies may compete on how well they interpret operational data rather than on visibility alone.
