OT Security Skill Trends 2026

An analysis of 500 U.S. OT and ICS security job postings: who is hiring, which skills employers ask for, and what they pay.

Port 502 Research analyzed 500 unique U.S. job postings for operational technology and industrial control systems security roles published between September 1 and December 1, 2026. The postings were collected from 120 unique company career pages and 30 job boards, including staffing firms and social sites.

01

Industry breakdown

Defense contractors, government and research organizations post the most OT security roles, ahead of utilities and manufacturers. AI data centers and crypto miners are a small but new group of employers, and they post the highest pay.

02

Seniority breakdown

OT security hiring favors experienced people. Just 4% of roles are entry level, and most of those are internships. Two-thirds of postings are at the mid, lead or principal level.

Individual contributors are expected to know the systems. At the entry through principal levels, roles ask for hands-on control system and protocol knowledge, plus scripting.

Leaders are expected to know governance. Manager and director postings shift toward policy, compliance and standards, and hands-on skills drop sharply.

03

Job titles and IT/OT breakdown

Of the 500 job postings we analyzed in full, 63% explicitly included “OT” in the job title. “OT” was the preferred label, appearing about four times more often than “ICS.”

The remaining 37% had no “OT” or “ICS” in the title, but still included OT responsibilities. These were hybrid IT/OT security roles, often advertised under generic security titles. Their descriptions included responsibilities such as securing control systems, segmenting plant networks, and supporting IEC 62443 programs.

Job seekers who search only for “OT security” may miss more than a third of relevant opportunities. Employers using generic security titles risk making OT-related positions harder for the specialists they want to attract to find.

04

Salary and pay

About half of the observed postings list a pay range. Pay figures below use the midpoint of each posted range. They show what employers advertise, not what people are actually paid.

The median posted midpoint is $157K. The middle half of roles fall between $125K and $186K.

The typical posting spans about $45K from the bottom end of the range to the top.

Pay by industry

The industry employees choose to work in matters more than their seniority or skills. AI data centers, defense and advanced energy post the highest ranges. Utilities and public employers post the lowest.

05

What raises pay

A security clearance has the strongest link to higher pay. Specialist skills (GRID certification, scripting, IEC 62443, PLC, penetration testing) also come with higher posted pay.

Two of the most-requested credentials come with lower posted pay. Employers that pay less, mainly utilities and compliance-heavy organizations, ask for them most. Certifications work as an entry requirement, not a pay lever.

06

Conclusion

OT security hiring is led by defense, utilities and manufacturers, and it favors experienced people. Entry-level roles are rare, individual contributors are hired for hands-on system knowledge, and leaders are hired for governance.

More than a third of OT roles don’t say “OT” in the title, so job seekers and employers alike need to look past the label.

On pay, the industry matters most. A security clearance and specialist skills raise posted pay, while common certifications get candidates in the door but don’t raise it.
